# Why this exists

For a long time I didn't consider a Garmin at all, and the reason was the map. Too many lines, too many labels, and at a complicated junction you still can't tell where to go.

I used to ride with the first Wahoo Roam. Its map was primitive: a few colours, almost no text. And it was enough. I looked at the screen less and at the road more. A friend I ride with would wave me to the front before every tricky junction, because he couldn't read his own screen and I could read mine.

When I finally started looking at Garmin, I found that people do make custom maps for it. But almost all of them go the other way: more detail, more labels, every footpath, full topo. I wanted a simpler map with strong contrast, where you see which road is which at a glance. Town names stay, because you want to know what's coming next. Street names go, because nobody reads them at speed.

So I built my own and gave the file to a friend to test on his Edge. He liked it, and then he started asking: could this be a different colour, could that have more contrast. Those were his preferences, not mine. That's when the map turned into a builder. Pick your own colours, check them on the preview, download the file. After that it's your map.

One rule I keep: the map should not take anything away from the device. Garmin's own map stays switched on underneath, so navigation and rerouting keep working.

It's free and the code is open. I wrote it with a lot of help from Claude Code. If something doesn't work on your device, open an issue.
